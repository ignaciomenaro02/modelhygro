Quantifying the hygrothermal contribution of bio- and geo-based walls to indoor comfort
and heating energy: a shoebox model benchmarked against Pleiades+COMFIE

Luis Ignacio Mena (1,3), Antonin Fabbri (1), Stephane Hans (1), Rudy Bui (2), Thibault Vialleton (3)

(1) LTDS UMR 5513, CNRS, ENTPE, 3 rue Maurice Audin, 69518 Vaulx-en-Velin cedex, France
(2) Universite Claude Bernard Lyon 1 (UCBL), 43 boulevard du 11 novembre 1918,
    69622 Villeurbanne cedex, France
(3) BATISERF Ingenierie, 11 boulevard Paul Langevin, 38600 Fontaine, France

Abstract (248 words)

Massive bio- and geo-based walls, such as hemp concrete or rammed earth, are often valued
for two properties: their thermal mass and their hygroscopicity, the ability to store and
exchange moisture with the room air. However, dynamic thermal tools used in France to
check RE2020 compliance, such as Pleiades+COMFIE, represent walls as
resistance-capacitance networks. They exchange sensible heat with the room air but not
moisture, so moisture buffering is left out of what these tools compute. This paper tries
to quantify that missing contribution.

A coupled heat-and-moisture single-zone shoebox model is being developed: four walls
solved with an implicit finite-difference scheme for the coupled
capillary-pressure/temperature state, a well-mixed room-air node, and the French RE2020
climate, occupancy, ventilation and heating scenarios. The same wall is simulated twice,
once with the moisture physics active and once deactivated as a control, walls
impermeable to vapour, sensible heat only; the gap isolates the hygrothermal
contribution. The vapour-closed case is also modelled in Pleiades+COMFIE, on the same
ground. Hygiene and night ventilation rates are varied too, since they drive moisture
exchange with the room. A one-year spin-up removes the initial bias of moisture storage.

The coupled model is expected to show a stabilisation of indoor humidity and less summer
discomfort in heat waves, compared to the vapour-closed case, depending on the
ventilation rate. Its effect on heating need is expected to be smaller. These results are
discussed for material choice in comfort-driven design and for the limits of thermal
tools with hygroscopic materials.

Keywords: bio-based materials, geo-based materials, hygrothermal simulation, moisture
buffering, ventilation, indoor comfort, heating energy, dynamic thermal simulation
